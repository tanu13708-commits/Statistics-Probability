# 04 — Sampling & Central Limit Theorem

## 25. What is a population vs a sample?

**Population:** Entire group we want to study.

**Sample:** A subset selected from the population.

Example: All students in a university = population. 200 selected students = sample.

---

## 26. What is sampling and why is it necessary?

Sampling means selecting a subset of a population for analysis.

It is necessary because studying an entire population may be:
- Expensive
- Time-consuming
- Impractical
- Sometimes impossible

A representative sample allows us to estimate population characteristics.

---

## 27. What are common sampling methods?

Common methods include:

1. **Simple Random Sampling** — every member has a known/equal chance of selection.
2. **Systematic Sampling** — select every kth member after a starting point.
3. **Stratified Sampling** — divide population into groups and sample from each.
4. **Cluster Sampling** — divide into clusters and select some clusters.
5. **Convenience Sampling** — select easily available participants.

---

## 28. What is sampling bias?

Sampling bias occurs when the sampling process systematically produces a sample that is not representative of the target population.

Example: Asking only gym members about exercise habits.

The problem is not necessarily the sample size; the selection process itself may be biased.

---

## 29. What is the Central Limit Theorem?

The Central Limit Theorem states that, under common conditions, the sampling distribution of the sample mean approaches a normal distribution as sample size becomes sufficiently large, even when the original population is not normally distributed.

The standard deviation of the sampling distribution of the mean is approximately:

**σ / √n**

This is called the standard error of the mean.

---

## 30. Why is the Central Limit Theorem important in statistics and ML?

CLT helps us:
- Approximate sampling distributions
- Construct confidence intervals
- Perform hypothesis tests
- Make population inferences from samples
- Understand why normal-based methods are widely useful

It is also useful for reasoning about averages of sampled data in ML experiments.
