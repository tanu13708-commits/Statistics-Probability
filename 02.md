# 02 — Random Variables & Distributions

## 9. What is a random variable?

A random variable is a function that assigns a numerical value to each outcome of a random experiment.

Example: In three coin tosses, X = number of Heads. Then X can be 0, 1, 2, or 3.

---

## 10. What is the difference between discrete and continuous random variables?

**Discrete random variable:** Takes countable values.

Examples:
- Number of students
- Number of defective products
- Number of heads

**Continuous random variable:** Can take any value within an interval.

Examples:
- Height
- Weight
- Temperature
- Time

---

## 11. What are PMF, PDF, and CDF?

### PMF — Probability Mass Function
Used for discrete random variables.

**P(X = x)** gives the probability that X equals x.

### PDF — Probability Density Function
Used for continuous random variables.

For a continuous variable, probability is represented by area under the curve.

### CDF — Cumulative Distribution Function
Gives the probability that X is less than or equal to x.

**F(x) = P(X ≤ x)**

---

## 12. What are expectation and variance?

**Expectation** is the long-run average or mean value of a random variable.

For a discrete variable:

**E[X] = Σ xP(X=x)**

**Variance** measures how spread out values are around the mean.

**Var(X) = E[(X - μ)²]**

Also:

**Var(X) = E[X²] - (E[X])²**

---

## 13. What is standard deviation?

Standard deviation measures the typical spread of observations around the mean.

**SD = √Variance**

A small standard deviation means observations are close to the mean; a large value means they are more spread out.

---

## 14. Explain the Bernoulli and Binomial distributions.

### Bernoulli Distribution

Models one experiment with exactly two outcomes:
- Success
- Failure

Example: One coin toss.

If success probability is p:

P(X=1)=p and P(X=0)=1-p.

### Binomial Distribution

Models the number of successes in **n independent Bernoulli trials** with the same success probability p.

**P(X=k) = C(n,k)p^k(1-p)^(n-k)**

Example: Number of Heads in 10 coin tosses.

---

## 15. Explain the Poisson distribution and when it is useful.

Poisson distribution models the number of times an event occurs in a fixed interval of time or space when events occur independently at an approximately constant average rate.

**P(X=k) = (e^-λ λ^k) / k!**

Here λ is the average number of occurrences.

Examples:
- Number of calls received per minute
- Number of website requests per second
- Number of defects per meter

---

## 16. What is the Normal distribution and why is it important in ML?

The Normal distribution is a continuous, symmetric, bell-shaped distribution defined by its mean μ and standard deviation σ.

It is important because:
- Many real-world measurements are approximately normal.
- The Central Limit Theorem leads many sampling distributions toward normality.
- It is used in statistical inference.
- Standardization commonly converts values into z-scores.

The standard normal distribution has mean 0 and standard deviation 1.
