# 03 — Descriptive Statistics

## 17. What are mean, median, and mode?

**Mean:** Arithmetic average.

Mean = Sum of values / Number of values

**Median:** Middle value after sorting the data.

**Mode:** Most frequently occurring value.

---

## 18. When is median preferable to mean?

Median is preferable when data contains strong outliers or is highly skewed.

Example: Salaries of 10 people where one person earns much more than everyone else. The mean can be pulled upward, while the median is more resistant.

---

## 19. What is range?

Range is the difference between the maximum and minimum values.

**Range = Maximum - Minimum**

It gives a simple measure of total spread.

---

## 20. What are variance and standard deviation?

Variance measures average squared deviation from the mean.

**Variance = Average of (x - mean)²**

Standard deviation is the square root of variance.

**SD = √Variance**

Variance is in squared units; standard deviation is in the original units.

---

## 21. What are percentiles and quartiles?

A **percentile** tells us the value below which a certain percentage of observations fall.

Example: 90th percentile means approximately 90% of observations are at or below that value.

Quartiles divide ordered data into four parts:
- Q1 = 25th percentile
- Q2 = 50th percentile / Median
- Q3 = 75th percentile

---

## 22. What is the interquartile range (IQR)?

IQR measures the spread of the middle 50% of data.

**IQR = Q3 - Q1**

It is less affected by extreme values than the range.

A common statistical outlier rule is:

- Lower bound = Q1 - 1.5 × IQR
- Upper bound = Q3 + 1.5 × IQR

---

## 23. What is skewness?

Skewness measures the asymmetry of a distribution.

- **Positive/right skew:** Long tail toward larger values.
- **Negative/left skew:** Long tail toward smaller values.
- **Approximately zero skewness:** Distribution is roughly symmetric.

---

## 24. What is kurtosis?

Kurtosis describes the heaviness of the tails and the concentration of observations around the center relative to a normal distribution.

High kurtosis generally indicates heavier tails and more extreme observations; low kurtosis indicates lighter tails.

In practice, kurtosis is often discussed using **excess kurtosis**, where a normal distribution has excess kurtosis of 0.
