# 07 — Common Statistical Tests

## 44. What is a t-test and when would you use it?

A t-test is used to compare means, especially when population standard deviation is unknown and sample size is relatively small under the assumptions of the test.

Common types:
- One-sample t-test
- Independent two-sample t-test
- Paired t-test

Example: Comparing the average scores of two groups.

---

## 45. What is a z-test?

A z-test is commonly used for testing a mean or proportion when the test statistic follows a standard normal framework, often when population standard deviation is known or sample size is sufficiently large under the relevant assumptions.

For a mean with known σ:

**z = (x̄ - μ₀) / (σ/√n)**

---

## 46. What is ANOVA?

ANOVA stands for **Analysis of Variance**.

It tests whether there is evidence that the means of three or more groups are not all equal.

The basic idea is to compare:
- Variation between groups
- Variation within groups

ANOVA can tell us that at least one group differs, but additional post-hoc tests are generally needed to identify which groups differ.

---

## 47. What is the chi-square test?

The chi-square test is commonly used for categorical data.

Two common applications:
- **Test of independence:** Checks whether two categorical variables are associated.
- **Goodness-of-fit:** Checks whether observed counts are consistent with expected counts.

The test uses differences between observed and expected frequencies.

---

## 48. When would you use a parametric vs non-parametric test?

**Parametric tests** generally make assumptions about population parameters/distributions.

Examples:
- t-test
- ANOVA

**Non-parametric tests** rely on fewer or different distributional assumptions.

Examples:
- Mann–Whitney U test
- Wilcoxon signed-rank test
- Kruskal–Wallis test

Choice depends on the data, design, scale, assumptions, and research question.

---

## 49. How would you choose an appropriate statistical test for a problem?

Consider:

1. What is the research question?
2. Is the target variable numerical or categorical?
3. How many groups are being compared?
4. Are observations independent or paired?
5. What assumptions are reasonable?
6. Is the data approximately normal where required?
7. Are variances comparable where required?
8. What sample size do we have?

Example:
- Two independent group means → often independent t-test.
- Three or more group means → often ANOVA.
- Two categorical variables → often chi-square test of independence.
- Paired numerical observations → often paired t-test.
