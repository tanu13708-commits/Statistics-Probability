# 08 — Correlation & Regression

## 50. What is covariance?

Covariance measures the direction in which two variables vary together.

**Cov(X,Y) = E[(X-μX)(Y-μY)]**

- Positive covariance → variables tend to increase together.
- Negative covariance → one tends to increase when the other decreases.
- Near-zero covariance → little linear co-movement.

Its magnitude depends on the units of the variables.

---

## 51. What is correlation?

Correlation is a standardized measure of the strength and direction of association between two variables.

Pearson correlation lies between **-1 and +1**.

- +1 → perfect positive linear relationship
- -1 → perfect negative linear relationship
- 0 → no linear correlation

---

## 52. Pearson vs Spearman correlation — what is the difference?

### Pearson
Measures **linear association** between numerical variables.

It is sensitive to outliers and is most appropriate when a linear relationship is meaningful.

### Spearman
Uses ranks and measures **monotonic association**.

It can be useful when:
- Relationship is monotonic but not linear
- Data is ordinal
- Outliers or non-normality make Pearson less appropriate

---

## 53. Why does correlation not imply causation?

Correlation shows that variables are associated, but it does not establish that one variable causes the other.

Possible explanations include:
- Reverse causation
- Confounding variables
- Coincidence

Example: Ice cream sales and swimming incidents may both increase in summer. Temperature is a possible confounding factor.

---

## 54. What is the relationship between correlation and linear regression?

Correlation measures the strength and direction of a linear association.

Linear regression models a relationship and can be used to estimate a response variable from one or more predictors.

For simple linear regression:

**y = β₀ + β₁x + ε**

Correlation and regression are related, but they have different purposes:
- Correlation is symmetric.
- Regression defines predictors and a response.

---

## 55. How can correlation be misleading?

Correlation can be misleading because of:

- Outliers
- Confounding variables
- Nonlinear relationships
- Restricted range
- Time trends
- Aggregated data

A correlation coefficient should therefore be interpreted together with plots and domain knowledge.
